# Deep research brief: Tipu Sultan and the Tipu Jayanti controversy

You are a research assistant fact-checking the script of a 13-minute YouTube explainer for an Indian general audience. The film examines Tipu Sultan (ruler of Mysore, 1782–1799) and the controversy over Karnataka's official "Tipu Jayanti" celebrations. It presents both the case for and the case against him, then argues that the state-sponsored celebration was politically motivated.

Your job is **not** to take a side. It is to establish, for each claim below, what the best evidence says, who says it, and how confident we can be. The film must be accurate enough to survive scrutiny from historians and from partisans on both sides.

## How to research

1. **Prefer scholarly and primary sources.** Academic books and journal articles by historians; published primary sources (Tipu's letters and their translations; contemporary British, Maratha, French and Persian accounts); museum and archive catalogues; government records; established newspapers for events since 2015. Use Wikipedia, blogs, opinion pieces and social media only as pointers to better sources, never as the citation.
2. **Cover every school of interpretation**, and say which school each source belongs to:
   - early British accounts (for example Wilks, Kirkpatrick, Beatson);
   - nationalist and Marxist reassessments (for example Mohibbul Hasan, Irfan Habib, B. Sheik Ali);
   - recent critical accounts (for example Vikram Sampath);
   - specialist academic work (for example Kate Brittlebank, Nikhiles Guha, Denys Forrest, Praxy Fernandes).
   Where these disagree, report each position fairly.
3. **For any number** (casualties, deportees, converts, temples), give the range found across sources, who gives each figure, and what it is based on. If a figure comes from Tipu's own boastful letters, say so.
4. **Separate three levels of certainty:** documented and broadly accepted; documented but disputed in scale or motive; asserted but weakly evidenced (oral tradition, a single partisan source, or no primary basis).
5. **Flag anything in the claims list that is wrong,** and give the correct version.
6. Where a source is available online (archive.org, JSTOR, Google Books, museum sites, newspaper archives), include the link. Give page numbers wherever possible.

## Claims to verify

### A. Life and reign
1. Tipu was born in 1750 at Devanahalli. Which birth date do the sources give (10 November and 20 November are both cited)? Why does Karnataka mark 10 November?
2. Hyder Ali was a soldier of humble origins who rose in the Wodeyar army and was the effective ruler of Mysore by the 1760s (which year is usually given?), while the Wodeyars kept the throne in name.
3. Hyder Ali was illiterate; Tipu was educated in languages, religion and mathematics and led cavalry as a teenager.
4. Tipu succeeded on Hyder's death in December 1782, during the Second Anglo-Mysore War, and ruled from Srirangapatna until his death on 4 May 1799.

### B. Wars against the East India Company
5. Dates and outcomes of the four Anglo-Mysore Wars (1767–69, 1780–84, 1790–92, 1798–99).
6. Battle of Pollilur, September 1780: was it "one of the worst defeats" of the Company in India? Size of the British force (Baillie's detachment) and its losses.
7. The Treaty of Seringapatam, 1792: did Tipu cede about half his territory and hand over two sons as hostages? Their names and ages.
8. Tipu died fighting during the storming of Srirangapatna in 1799 and refused to surrender. What do eyewitness accounts say?
9. The Marathas and the Nizam of Hyderabad were allied with the Company in the Third War (and the Nizam in the Fourth).

### C. Rockets and objects
10. Mysorean iron-cased rockets: their design, how they were used at Pollilur and later, and their influence on William Congreve's rocket (Congreve's own 1807 account). Are captured Mysore rockets held in UK collections (for example the Royal Artillery Museum or Firepower collection)? Where are they now?
11. Tipu's Tiger: the automaton with an organ, held by the Victoria and Albert Museum. Catalogue details.

### D. Administration
12. Land revenue reforms (direct collection, removal of intermediaries), new coinage, weights and measures, and his own calendar.
13. State trading houses or factories, and attempts to build a navy.
14. Sericulture: is the Mysore silk industry credibly traced to Tipu? What is the evidence?
15. Embassies to France (Louis XVI, 1787–88) and to the Ottoman Sultan; any mission to Kabul (Zaman Shah). Dates and outcomes.
16. Purnaiah: a Brahmin who served Hyder and Tipu as a senior finance official and later became Diwan under the Wodeyars. Other Hindus in high office.

### E. Persecution: the case against
17. **Kodagu (Coorg), 1780s:** campaigns, deportations to Srirangapatna, forced conversions. Figures given by Tipu's letters and by historians; how reliable each is.
18. **Malabar, about 1788–1790:** forced conversions (especially of Nairs), destruction of temples, flight of Hindus to Travancore. Which temples are documented? Which letters of Tipu are cited (for example the letter to Budruz Zuman Khan, 1790), and is their authenticity accepted?
19. **Mangalorean Catholics, 1784–1799:** the "captivity at Seringapatam". The range of estimates for those deported (sources cite roughly 30,000 to 80,000), deaths and conversions. The basis for each estimate.
20. Other communities sometimes cited: the Mandyam Iyengars of Melkote (the Naraka Chaturdashi mourning tradition), the Nairs, and others. Assess the evidence for each as documented, disputed, or oral tradition.
21. Did Tipu describe any of his campaigns as jihad or holy war in his own writings? Give quotations with sources.
22. Renaming of places (for example Mangalore to Jalalabad, Mysore to Nazarabad). Documented?

### F. Patronage: the case for
23. **Sringeri, 1791:** the Maratha raid on the Sringeri math (by Raghunath Rao Patwardhan's forces?), and Tipu's response. How many letters from Tipu to the Shankaracharya survive? When were they found and published (Mysore Archaeological Department Annual Report, 1916?) and by whom (R. Narasimhachar?)? What do they say?
24. Grants to other temples: Melkote, Nanjangud (the "Padshah linga"), Kanchi, Srirangapatna's Ranganathaswamy temple. Which are documented?
25. The argument that Tipu's harshness tracked rebellion and suspected collaboration with the British more than religion alone. Who makes it (for example Brittlebank, Hasan), and who rejects it?

### G. Historiography
26. Mark Wilks, *Historical Sketches of the South of India* (1810–1817), and William Kirkpatrick, *Select Letters of Tippoo Sultan* (1811): their portrayal of Tipu, and modern assessments of their bias.
27. Mohibbul Hasan, *History of Tipu Sultan* (1951): its argument and reception.
28. Kate Brittlebank, *Tipu Sultan's Search for Legitimacy* (1997) and *Tiger: The Life of Tipu Sultan* (2016): main argument.
29. Irfan Habib (ed.), *Confronting Colonialism* (1999): main argument.
30. Vikram Sampath, *Tipu Sultan: The Saga of the Mysore Interregnum* (2024): main argument and reception.
31. Any other major recent works or reviews, and any notable public debates between historians.

### H. Tipu Jayanti: the modern controversy
32. When and by which government was official Tipu Jayanti introduced in Karnataka (10 November 2015, Siddaramaiah's Congress government?). The stated reasons.
33. The 2015 protests: where they turned violent, who died (in Madikeri, Kodagu?), and the circumstances, as reported by established newspapers.
34. Cancellation in 2019 (by B. S. Yediyurappa's BJP government, July 2019?): the stated reasons. Later court cases.
35. Was Tipu Jayanti celebrated officially, or by private groups, in 2023, 2024, 2025 and November 2026? By whom?
36. The arguments made at the time by critics (including the "appeasement" or "vote bank" charge) and by supporters. Quote named politicians, organisations and commentators on both sides, with dates.
37. Which Muslim organisations and leaders championed Tipu as a community icon, and since when? What do they say about why he matters to them?
38. A list of the official jayantis Karnataka celebrates (for example Basava, Kempegowda, Valmiki, Kanakadasa and others), with the year each was introduced and the government that introduced it. Have commentators described the jayanti system as community or caste politics, regardless of party?
39. Related controversies, for example Tipu in Karnataka school textbooks (revisions around 2022) and the Tipu statue debates. Brief summary with dates.

## Output format

Write a report in Markdown with these parts:

1. **Executive summary** (half a page): the five most important corrections or cautions for the script.
2. **Claim table:** one row per numbered claim above, with these columns:
   `#` · `Claim (short)` · `Verdict` (Confirmed / Confirmed with correction / Disputed / Weakly evidenced / Wrong) · `Best figure or wording to use on screen` · `Sources` (author, title, year, page, link) · `Notes` (including which school of interpretation each source belongs to).
3. **Disputed figures:** for each number (claims 6, 17, 18, 19, 20 and any others), a small table of every estimate, who gives it, and on what basis.
4. **Key quotations:** up to ten short, verbatim, correctly attributed quotations useful on screen (from Tipu's letters, the Sringeri letters, British accounts, and modern historians from each school), each with a full citation.
5. **Timeline:** a dated list of 1750–1799 and 2015–2026 events, with a source for each.
6. **Bibliography:** every source used, grouped as primary, scholarly secondary, journalism, and other.
7. **Gaps and cautions:** anything you could not verify, sources you could not access, and any places where the script risks overstating the evidence.

Be precise and neutral. When the evidence is uncertain, say so plainly rather than choosing a side.
