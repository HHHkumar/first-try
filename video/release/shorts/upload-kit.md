## How to upload (YouTube Shorts)

1. Upload the `.mp4` from YouTube Studio or the YouTube app. Anything vertical and under 3 minutes is treated as a Short.
2. **Title:** paste the title below. Keep `#shorts` at the end; it does no harm and helps on older apps.
3. **Description:** paste the description, then the common footer at the end of this file.
4. **Related video:** in Studio → the Short → *Related video*, pick the matching long-form video. This puts a tappable link to the full video on the Short itself. It is the single most useful setting for sending Shorts viewers to the long videos.
5. **Cover:** YouTube lets you pick a frame from the Short in the mobile app (Edit → Thumbnail). The `cover-N.png` images are for Instagram, Facebook and your website; on YouTube, choose a frame from the first 2 seconds, where the hook headline is large.
6. **Comments:** post the pinned comment from your own account and pin it.
7. **Audience:** Not made for kids. **Category:** Education. **Language:** English.
8. **Altered or synthetic content:** the narration is your own cloned voice; no label is required, but the footer credits say so.

**Schedule suggestion:** one Short a day for nine days, at the time your channel analytics show the most viewers. Post them in the order of the series (1 → 9), so the "Related video" links follow the playlist order.

**Playlists:** add each Short to a playlist named **Electrical MCQs in 90 seconds**.

**Instagram Reels / Facebook Reels:** upload the same `.mp4` with the caption given, and choose `cover-N.png` as the cover. Put the link `electricalmcqs.in/lab` in your bio or link sticker, since links in Reels captions are not clickable.

The electrons in your charger cable drift at less than a millimetre a second. A snail would overtake them. So how does a lamp light the instant you flick the switch?

The push travels down the wire at nearly the speed of light, and every electron starts moving at once. As they move, they crash into the metal's atoms: that is resistance, and it is why heaters get hot and fuses melt.

## 5. Why switches spark

**Title (YouTube):** Why you sometimes see a spark when you unplug something #shorts

**Description:**
```
That spark when you pull a plug is a coil refusing to let go. Coils hate any change in current: switch on, and the current climbs slowly as the magnetic field builds; switch off, and the collapsing field drives the current on, building up thousands of volts until a spark jumps the gap.

This is inductance, measured in henries. The same jolt fires the spark plugs in a petrol engine.

## 9. The current that runs late

**Title (YouTube):** Why current lags voltage in a coil (and the CIVIL trick) #shorts

**Description:**
```
Put a coil on an AC supply and the current arrives a quarter of a cycle late. In a capacitor it arrives a quarter of a cycle early. In a resistor they keep perfect step.

Remember it with one word: CIVIL. In a capacitor (C), current (I) comes before voltage (V). In an inductor (L), voltage (V) comes before current (I). And because of that gap, coils and capacitors limit current without getting warm: reactance.

▶ Full explainer: Out of Step (linked above)
